# Oleshky under blockade: a fact sheet

*Oleshky (Олешки), Kherson oblast, Ukraine · as of **25 September 2026** · a living document; [corrections are welcome](https://github.com/vestacore/river-portal/issues)*

River Portal is built on one rule: **the left bank is always open**. Anyone in need may ask for help, and help may reach them. In Oleshky, a town on the left bank of the Dnipro opposite Kherson, that rule has been broken by force. This fact sheet sets out what is documented, who has said what, and what is not yet verified. Every statement links to a dated source.

> **Our position.** We, the authors of River Portal, regard the deliberate starvation and entrapment of Oleshky's civilian population by the Russian Federation as **genocide**, and we say so plainly. This is our position. International bodies have so far used narrower legal terms; the section [What officials and organisations say](#what-officials-and-organisations-say) sets them out exactly.

## At a glance
- An occupied town opposite Kherson; about **24,000** residents before 2022 [1][7].
- Ukrainian officials estimate that about **2,000** people remain in the town, and up to **6,000** in the wider area, including about **180–200 children** [1][2][3].
- **No food has been delivered since 26 May 2026**, according to Ukrainian officials; the UN confirmed there had been none as of 24 June [2][3].
- The UN human rights mission recorded at least **29 civilians killed and 54 injured** in Oleshky and Hola Prystan in 2026, up to late June, mostly by drones [2].
- Roads are **mined and watched by drones**; there have been **no evacuations since 26 May**, and people leave on foot along mined roads [2][3][4].
- Ukraine says Russia has not confirmed an evacuation mechanism [5]. The Kremlin acknowledges critical shortages but blames Ukraine [6][7].

## Timeline
- **February 2022**: Oleshky is occupied in the first days of the full-scale invasion [8].
- **6 June 2023**: The Russian-held Kakhovka dam is destroyed, and each side blames the other [9]. About 90% of Oleshky floods and the occupation authorities seal the town [10]. An AP investigation found that at least hundreds of people died there; Russia's official toll for all the areas it held was 59 [11].
- **December 2025**: A full blockade begins, according to residents, volunteers and an EU feature [12][8].
- **March–April 2026**: Ukraine's Ombudsman, Dmytro Lubinets, asks Russia's ombudsperson and the ICRC for a humanitarian corridor; Russia does not reply [13][14].
- **9–11 May**: Volunteers evacuate 32 people during a lull [2].
- **26 May**: The last food delivery and the last evacuation [2][3].
- **25 June**: The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission (HRMMU) urges a local ceasefire [2].
- **12 August**: Lubinets says an evacuation mechanism has been negotiated; Russia has not acted on it [15].
- **21–23 September**: The Kremlin acknowledges shortages [6]; Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha raises Oleshky at the UN Security Council [16].

## The blockade today (as of 25 September 2026)
- **Leaving.** People who left since March told Human Rights Watch they passed Russian checkpoints at soldiers' discretion, with phone checks [17]. The Insider reported "filtration" [18]. Oleshky's Ukrainian military administrator, Tetiana Hasanenko, says the barriers are mines, shelling and drones rather than a formal ban [19]. Some residents walk out along mined roads [3][5].
- **Aid.** Hasanenko says no vehicle has entered since 26 May [20]; Suspilne reported that an ambulance reached the town on 17 September [21]. The Kherson regional head, Oleksandr Prokudin, says Ukraine sends aid by drone and that Russian forces shoot it down [22].
- **Shortages.** There is no mains electricity, gas or heating, and drinking water is scarce [13][23][24]. The hospital lacks generator fuel and food [4]. Residents report eating garden weeds [24].
- **Deaths.** A volunteer's list counts more than 300 deaths from cold, hunger and attacks since autumn 2025 [12], and about 1,200 since 2022 [24]; neither figure has been independently verified. On 20 September a drone killed a couple carrying food who could not get home before a Russian-imposed curfew, the BBC reported [24].

## What officials and organisations say
- **Ukraine.** Ombudsman Lubinets calls the situation deliberate "terrorism" [13] and says 358 people have been verified as needing urgent evacuation [5]. The foreign ministry describes Russian crimes that flagrantly violate humanitarian law [1].
- **United Nations.** The HRMMU cannot conclusively attribute individual drone attacks [2]. On 22 September its head, Danielle Bell, said the mission had no information on why discussions had stalled [4]. The humanitarian coordinator, Matthias Schmale, said a local "window of silence" would be enough for aid and evacuation [25].
- **OSCE/ODIHR.** Witnesses said Russian forces stopped civilians from leaving and used them to shield positions; ODIHR says this, if established, may amount to the **war crime of using human shields** [23].
- **Human Rights Watch.** Russia should not arbitrarily stop departures or tie them to Russian passports, and both sides should stop using antipersonnel mines [17].
- **ICRC.** It is in dialogue with both sides [26] and ready to help "if requested by the parties when the appropriate security guarantees are in place" [7].
- **Other voices.** Residents told the Ukrainian NGO MIHR that Russian aid went only to holders of Russian passports [27]. An EU (EEAS) web feature cites local claims of more than 300 deaths [8].
- **Russia.** The Russian-installed governor, Vladimir Saldo, denied a crisis in May and rejected an evacuation ceasefire [28]. On 21 September the Kremlin spokesman, Dmitry Peskov, said the military was tackling shortages and accused Ukraine of obstructing deliveries with drones [6][7].
- **The words used.** At the time of writing, no official or international body has formally called the situation in Oleshky *genocide*. Foreign Minister Sybiha invoked the Holodomor as a historical parallel [16]. The only international finding of a possible *war crime* is ODIHR's conditional one above [23]. The UN Commission of Inquiry's 2025 finding of *crimes against humanity* concerned drone attacks on Ukrainian-held Kherson on the right bank, not Oleshky [29].

## What is not yet verified
- **Population.** 1,500–3,000 people in the town and 6,000–7,000 in the wider area; about 50 children in the town and 180–200 overall. All figures come from Ukrainian officials or volunteers [1][2][3][18][19][22]; restricted access prevents independent checks [7].
- **Deaths** from hunger and cold [24][28], and **who operates the drones** that hit the roads [2][3][7].
- **The 2 June convoy.** One person was killed by a landmine according to the HRMMU [2]; two drivers were killed by Russian drones according to the Kyiv Independent [3].
- **The evacuation mechanism.** Russia has not confirmed the mechanism Ukraine says was agreed [5][15], and has shown no verifiable evidence of regular deliveries since May [28].

## What you can do
- **Share only verified, attributed information**, like this fact sheet, and never identify people who are still inside [17].
- **Support the calls for access**: the HRMMU's call for a local ceasefire [2] and the security guarantees the ICRC requires [7].
- **Give only to established humanitarian organisations.** No reputable source names a channel that currently reaches Oleshky.
- **Ask your elected representatives** to press for humanitarian access and evacuation.
- **If you are a witness**, report to Ukraine's Ombudsman [13][30], to the UN human rights mission [2] or to the OSCE/ODIHR monitors [23].
